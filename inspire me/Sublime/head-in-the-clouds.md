Every company begins with a dream. 

Ours was to build the internet we wish existed. 

Building a personal knowledge management tool is just where that dream met the ground.

The way humans discover and share ideas has changed across centuries, but one thing hasn't: what ideas can do for our minds. Ideas remain the most powerful vehicle to change the world.

For most of human history, we passed knowledge through stories and speech. When lost, it was lost forever.
Then came writing, making ideas permanent.
Then the printing press, making them accessible.
Then, in 1991, the web arrived.

For the first time in history, a dial-up connection became a portal to humanity's collective knowledge. Curiosity—not geography—determined what you could learn.
For a moment, it was beautiful.
The internet promised to realize the ancient dream of a library without walls. 

Instead, we built digital casinos.
We turned attention into currency.
Distraction into profit. 
We built algorithms optimized for engagement, not enlightenment.
Performance, not reflection. 

The world's brightest minds spent a decade futzing with ad technology while the dream of organizing human knowledge gathered dust. The dream of infinite knowledge became the reality of infinite scrolling.
Just because the web hasn’t been a place for living intentionally, doesn’t mean it can’t be.

Another world is possible.

Where saving one insight reveals a hundred more.
