# Daimler
I open the window just a crack and lean my cheeks against the glass. This world between destinations – this is my quiet place. As the evening air pours in, I close my eyes.

Here, in a spa of comfort, I pay respect to my time. Strong body, soft heart, I chant, letting the stars above peel through the dead skin of the office. If I listen closely, I can hear myself in the wind against my face. Reaching inward, I feel a light begin to glow.

Holding my North Star in view, I let the magic carpet be my conductor.  
**This is Daimler.**

# Worldcoin
When the state of being and everyday human transaction generates value of its own, we believe people may find the bandwidth to better uncover and understand the value that is more inherent within themselves.

There is a mantra on the back of the dollar bill: *in god we trust.* Was the mantra meant to be repeated during each monetary transaction? Was a dollar bill designed as a single rosary bead, each intended to keep you on track as you paid your way through life? Were we believers before we were consumers? What did we believe in? Were purchases prayers? How do you feel about the last five dollars you spent? Can you remember?

**This is Worldcoin.**

# ARK 1
If a monolith falls over in the desert for no one to see or hear it, did it ever really happen? My answer is *yes.* I know it does because I can feel it. I can feel it in the air.

I can feel it in my hopes and dreams. I can feel it in the voices of my friends. I can feel it in the news. I can feel it in my mushrooms and my LSD. I can feel it in my soul.

I never felt it as strongly as I do now. Something happened. One day soon, I’ll be driving up the I-5 in the middle of the night and I’ll see a rectangular glow in the distance.

When this happens, I’ll throw my iPhone out the window and never look back.  
**This is ARK 1.**

# LOT
There were vehicles to get me to where I was going. There was money in my pocket to pay for my next cup of coffee. There were homes that would let me in and beds upon which I would lay my head and drift off to sleep.

Sometimes there were stars above me. There were clothes on my body and even more hanging out on the clothesline to dry. There were parties to attend and people to see. This is what you call life, but there is something that elevates it—something that threads us all together to create something new.

I get to the club and embrace my friend. *Be still.* It is love.  
**This is LOT.**

# Snap
The first time our eyes ever opened — the first time we could see — what was it that we thought we were looking through? What was this contraption through which images so beautifully and accurately presented themselves?

We were too astounded to make any kind of guess, but one thing was certain: it was the most incredible tool we had ever used.  

**This is Snap.**

# YEEZY
In an instant, he created the heavens and earth. It wasn’t initially earth as we know it, or heaven as we imagine it. At first, it was formless and dark.

I always wondered why he didn’t introduce light from the very beginning. Maybe he needed the dark for a while. To be alone with time and space. To think about what would come next. Maybe he was afraid to see what he created, once it was all inevitably revealed by the light. He would take as long as needed.

For now, time was the greatest tool he knew.  
**This is YEEZY.**

# KKW
Light was born from darkness. A small sliver at first and then a catapulting flood, colliding with surfaces and refracting back — from the rivers of Armenia to the mountains of Calabasas.

The world was illuminated, but we couldn’t comprehend it — *a jupiter and the infinite moment.* Nothing that the light revealed made sense until it landed on the one thing we could all instantly recognize: a face.  

**This is KKW.**

# Samara
You wanna know what’s going to happen the day after tomorrow? You’re gonna wake up on a bus. It’s coming to a slow and bumpy stop. It’s the end of the line.

Your forehead’s cold from resting on the glass all night, but you know it’s warm outside. Because you’re in Los Angeles. As soon as you step off the bus, you remember everything—the way the air lifts you off your feet and cradles you to the lullaby of swaying palms and howling wolves.

You can’t remember how long you’ve been gone, or if you ever left. Before you check into your Airbnb or hotel or Ark or whatever you booked, you take another long breath in and out. You feel weightless. Of the things you packed and the things you didn’t. Of the distance ahead and the distance behind you. Of the things that will change and the things that won’t. And for whatever it’s worth, you feel you belong.  

**This is Samara.**

# Google
I used to do this thing. I’d store all my data on my friend’s computer and in turn, she’d store her data on mine. So we’d never lose it. The data, I mean. I don’t really speak to that friend anymore. I wonder if she’s still storing my data. Or did she delete it? Or did she involuntarily lose everything when/if her computer stopped working?

But whatever.  

**This is Google.**

# Lapka
Technology was so good it knew when to disappear. And death, almost magically, went along with it hand in hand. But I’m still getting older. I was 16 when it all began. I’ll be 50 in the year 2046. My glucose levels are still kind of high.

At least I think. I haven’t checked in a while. I won’t need a device to know that everything is going to be okay.  

**This is Lapka.**
